# Earworm topic audit and candidate slate

October 2, 2026. Editorial assessment of the captured production inventory. No queue, publication, or production configuration changes are made by this document.

The strongest objection to simply adding broader interests is that Earworm already has them. The September 21 interests file names ten substantial non-AI domains. The next improvement needs to preserve those interests through selection, queue ordering, research, and archive comparison. Otherwise different nouns keep producing the same episode about an apparent breakthrough, a hidden bottleneck, and limits in its evidence.

## Where breadth gets lost

These observations describe the captured implementation, before any changes from this audit.

- **The history window forgets substantial coverage.** `recent_coverage(limit=80)` includes only the newest 80 episode rows and 80 topic rows. The old CUDA, cable, and liability stories are outside that window. A candidate can pass the current gate while repeating an older episode closely.
- **The rotation sees a different sequence from the listener.** `recent_coverage()` puts episode rows first and all topic statuses afterward. Its first ten entries ignore pending playback order, include other feeds and unpublished ledger rows, and are not interleaved with scheduled topics. Failed topics count as coverage for duplicate screening even if a listener never heard them.
- **A batch can defeat its own diversity.** Discovery requests the shortfall plus three candidates and queues every surviving candidate. The semantic gate compares candidates with history, not with each other. Topics #161 and #162 were proposed together, then became consecutive episodes about the same restarted reactor and interconnection dispute.
- **The lane instructions conflict at the boundary.** Captured `interests.md` forbids exceeding a lane's target; captured `autogen.md` forbids proposing a lane already at its target. A perfectly balanced 2/2/2/4 window therefore leaves no eligible lane under the latter instruction. Planning the next slot requires dropping the oldest entry and considering the resulting window.
- **AI can occupy nominally non-AI lanes.** AI hardware fits C; automation and data-center power fit D. A lane count alone can satisfy the stated proportions while the listener keeps hearing about AI. The two newest power episodes illustrate this route.
- **Topic wording often announces the verdict before research.** #156 assumes construction productivity falls while every other sector rises; #159 asserts its numbers and primary record are already settled; #161 declares the real bottleneck. These are research questions with answers embedded in them. They invite the next stage to defend a premise or produce another debunk when it fails.

## Selection policy for the next pass

Keep the existing interests recognizable. Repair selection before adding a new recommender service or another editorial model pass.

1. Give discovery the recent **rendered default-feed sequence**, upcoming runnable topics in playback order, and a separate **full archive** of title plus specific payoff. The local implementation uses rendered episodes as its available history and labels that scope; it does not infer publication or listening. Failed/unrendered leads may deserve revival, but must not occupy a rendered-history slot. A publication-based sequence could refine this later if its completeness is established.
2. Choose candidates sequentially against the projected sequence, comparing each survivor with earlier survivors in the same batch. A different title, lane label, or money-versus-mechanism framing cannot rescue the same central case and listener payoff.
3. Retain the existing 2/2/2/4 lane targets and aim for at least four genuinely non-AI episodes in a projected ten. Count AI-centered hardware, labor, power, and policy stories as AI regardless of lane; a C or D label does not make them non-AI. Keep the existing one-Anthropic-in-five cap.
4. Within the non-AI portion, spread subjects across the existing subdomains. Avoid consecutive episodes about the same case or mechanism, even when they would be classified differently. Permit a deliberate continuation when the new evidence and payoff are explicit.
5. Commission a question with a bounded scope and a promising source trail. Do not require the hook to be a hidden failure, an exposed marketing claim, or a paradox. A well-explained working system, an ingenious solution, and a consequential historical decision can each sustain an episode.
6. Treat shapes as possibilities, not slots. The evidence should decide whether an episode follows a timeline, a comparison, a transaction, a physical process, or an argument. Recent repetition is a reason to inspect the choice, not to impose an arbitrary different outline.

The practical test is a run of several varied, source-supported commissions and finished episodes that Tanner wants to hear. Inventory breadth, passing duplicate checks, or a balanced table cannot establish that on their own.
